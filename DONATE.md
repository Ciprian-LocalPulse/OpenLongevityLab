# Support OpenLongevity

OpenLongevity is open scientific infrastructure: provenance-preserving evidence records, transparent scoring, and reproducible research tooling for the biology of aging. It is built in the open and stays free to use, fork, and audit.

Maintaining it costs real time and money — data-adapter upkeep, server and database costs, CI/CD, security review, and the hours spent keeping the evidence pipeline correct and trustworthy. If OpenLongevity is useful to you, in your research, your product, or your own learning, please consider supporting its development.

**What your support funds:**
- Keeping the PubMed, Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Crossref, and ClinicalTrials.gov adapters running and up to date
- Infrastructure: PostgreSQL hosting, CI pipelines, staging environments
- Time spent on evidence-scoring accuracy, contradiction surfacing, and reproducibility
- Documentation, onboarding, and community review

Financial support is entirely optional. It does not purchase scientific conclusions, priority review, or access to private research data — see [`RESEARCH_ETHICS.md`](RESEARCH_ETHICS.md) and [`GOVERNANCE.md`](GOVERNANCE.md) for how the project keeps funding and findings separate.

Every contribution, of any size, genuinely helps and is appreciated.

## Funding scope and present project status

OpenLongevity is maintained by Ciprian Ștefan Pleșca, an independent Romanian researcher. Financial support can help sustain the practical work of maintaining open research software, but the project remains a prototype with partially integrated infrastructure. A contribution does not establish that a planned feature has been delivered or that an experimental method has been scientifically validated. The [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) and [audit record](docs/audits/v0.3.0-baseline.md) distinguish current evidence from intended development.

Maintenance includes work that is easy to overlook in a demonstration. Provider formats change, difficult records expose parser limitations, database behavior needs integration tests, and documentation must remain consistent with code. These activities improve the conditions under which a researcher can inspect a result. They are different from proving a biological discovery, and funding communication should not confuse infrastructure improvement with demonstrated health benefit.

The support channels above are supplied by the project author. This document does not claim that a payment transaction has been tested or completed by the documentation audit. No transfer is required to read, inspect, or contribute to the repository. Payment processing takes place through the selected channel and its own interface; the research software does not need donor payment credentials to perform evidence navigation.

## What a useful maintenance outcome looks like

A funded maintenance outcome should be described through a concrete artifact or behavior. Examples include a parser regression corrected against a representative fixture, a migration path exercised on an isolated database, an executable example repaired, or a misleading capability statement corrected. Such outcomes can be examined independently. A general claim that the platform is now trustworthy is too broad without identifying the checks and their limitations.

Infrastructure work should also have an observable boundary. Configuring a service, producing a successful deployment, testing recovery, and establishing ongoing operating capacity are different achievements. The project should report which occurred rather than imply that one payment guarantees indefinite hosting or availability. Planned expenditure categories are not an audited account of past spending, and this page does not invent a financial ledger or budget that has not been published.

Documentation support is valuable when it makes methods and limitations easier to understand. The current expansion targets substantive explanations, corrected code examples, explicit provenance, and interpretable diagrams. A word-count threshold is an editorial minimum, not a scientific-quality certificate. Support for that work should be assessed through the actual documents and verification artifacts rather than through the appearance of academic language alone.

## Separation between funding and evidence

A donor does not purchase a favorable evidence grade, a positive research conclusion, or a verified review status. Scientific interpretation remains constrained by the source, study design, and appropriate evaluation. The same standard applies whether a topic is financially supported, commercially attractive, or personally important to a contributor. A payment should not cause negative or inconclusive evidence to disappear from the research record.

Sponsorship that materially relates to a project claim should be disclosed with enough context for readers to assess it. Disclosure identifies an interest; it does not replace methodological review. The project should not describe a donor as an institutional partner, scientific reviewer, or endorser without an actual corresponding relationship. Project visibility and scientific responsibility are distinct, and neither should be inferred merely from financial support.

Requests for new features can be discussed through the normal contribution and governance process. Their acceptance depends on scope, feasibility, maintenance capacity, and evidence requirements. This page does not promise a delivery date, priority scientific review, or a contractual service level. An agreed professional engagement, if one is established separately, would need its own explicit scope rather than be inferred from a voluntary contribution.

## Responsible communication with supporters

Progress updates should identify completed work and remaining limitations. A passing parser test is useful, but it does not demonstrate complete provider coverage. A working publication database is useful, but it does not establish a clinical decision system. The project should communicate those distinctions clearly, including when a planned milestone remains blocked by missing evaluation or deployment evidence.

The author designation on this page is the project's actual independent status. Aspirations for rigorous research do not imply affiliation with a university, government laboratory, or funding institution. Supporters should be able to evaluate the work without relying on invented credentials, partnerships, user counts, or impact statistics. Corrections to an earlier claim should remain visible rather than be hidden to preserve a promotional narrative.

## Nonfinancial support and useful scrutiny

Reproducible criticism is a valuable contribution. A report that identifies an incorrect constructor, an unsupported provenance claim, or a statistical edge case can materially improve the project. Include the affected revision, expected behavior, and a minimal appropriate example. Use synthetic data where real personal or restricted information is unnecessary. Sensitive vulnerabilities belong in the private reporting path described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

Other useful contributions include reviewing a methodological assumption, improving a diagram's clarity, checking a source citation, or reproducing a documented workflow. Credit should describe the actual work rather than imply coauthorship or endorsement without agreement. The [contribution guide](CONTRIBUTING.md) explains the review process, and [governance](GOVERNANCE.md) identifies current decision responsibility. The aim is a sustainable project whose value can be inspected regardless of the size or form of support.
